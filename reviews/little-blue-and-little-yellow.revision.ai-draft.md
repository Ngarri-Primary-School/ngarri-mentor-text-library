# Little Blue and Little Yellow - complete revision draft

Status: `ai_suggested` - awaiting teacher approval  
Evidence: complete 48-page school Drive PDF and visually checked story pages  
Author/illustrator: Leo Lionni

## Classification

**Text type:** Narrative, multimodal picture book.  
**Genre:** Conceptual friendship story and visual allegory.  
**Rationale:** A simple chronological problem-and-resolution narrative is carried jointly by spare sentences and torn-paper colour forms. The characters' visible colour change works literally within the story while also opening questions about friendship, identity, belonging and recognition.

## Writing connections

### Ideas - Foundation
**Key Understanding:** "writing is used to communicate a message/meaning/idea"  
**Key Skill:** "I can convey my own ideas through talking, drawing and writing."  
**In this book:** Lionni communicates friendship and belonging through both the short narration and the blue, yellow and green paper forms. At the joyful hug, the words name the action while the overlapping colours show its consequence. Students can notice that an idea can be communicated through a deliberate partnership of drawing and writing, then plan one message that both modes help express.

### Organisation - Foundation
**Key Understanding:** "writing is sequenced"  
**Key Skill:** "I can write in a logical sequence."  
**In this book:** The narrative moves clearly from introducing the two friends, to little blue's search, their transformation, rejection at both homes, their separation into tears and the parents' discovery. Students can reorder selected images or events and explain how each event causes the next before composing a short beginning-middle-end sequence of their own.

### Organisation - Year 1
**Key Understanding:** "writing has organisational features and these features assist the reader to understand the message"  
**Key Skill:** "I can explain the purpose of organisational features."  
**In this book:** Lionni distributes small actions across separate pages and reserves whole pages for decisive visual changes. The search unfolds through the repeated locations "here", "there" and "everywhere", while the hug and green transformation receive separate beats. Students can explain how page turns, repetition and image placement slow anticipation and make the transformation easy to understand.

### Word Choice - Year 2
**Key Understanding:** "rhymes, rhythm and word patterns can be used to enhance the reader's experience"  
**Key Skill:** "I can identify and use memorable words and phrases."  
**In this book:** Repeated patterns such as "here and there and everywhere", "hugged each other and hugged each other", and "cried and cried" create an oral rhythm and stretch important actions. The idiom "pulled themselves together" is also made literal when the scattered tears reform as the two children. Students can collect these patterns, explain their effect, and adapt one repetition or double meaning in a new scene.

### Audience/Voice - Year 2
**Key Understanding:** "writing can capture a general mood and evoke emotions such as joy, fear, excitement and humour."  
**Key Skill:** "I can explain and justify ways I can engage my audience (e.g interesting leads, word choice, onomatopoeia, persuasive devices etc)."  
**In this book:** The narrator shifts from the warm exclamation about the friends' games, to the searching rhythm, the parents' repeated rejection and the relief of "but look" when the adults also become green. Students can identify how repetition, exclamation and brief direct speech guide the audience through anticipation, sadness and joy without lengthy emotional description.

### Sentence Fluency - Year 1
**Key Understanding:** "my sentences need to sound smooth and fluent when I read them aloud."  
**Key Skill:** "I can re-read my sentences to check they sound smooth, fluent and maintain correct tense."  
**In this book:** Most sentences are short and easy to voice, but Lionni varies them with lists, repetition, exclamations and the longer sentence that carries the search around a corner to its discovery. Reading the search, hug and crying passages aloud lets students hear how phrasing and repeated conjunctions control pace, then revise a short passage for smoother oral reading.

### Presentation - Foundation
**Key Understanding:** "visuals can enhance my writing"  
**Key Skill:** "I can draw a picture which matches my writing."  
**In this book:** A blue shape, a yellow shape and their green overlap make the characters and central event immediately readable. The illustrations do more than decorate the sentences: their position, colour and overlap show home, movement, emotion and transformation. Students can match a sentence to the visual information it needs and create a picture that makes that sentence clearer.

### Presentation - Year 1
**Key Understanding:** "visuals can enhance my writing"  
**Key Skill:** "I can select/create visuals that will enhance the reader's understanding."  
**In this book:** Lionni deliberately changes scale, placement, background and colour to represent action: little blue appears tiny beside home, moves across successive search pages, overlaps little yellow, becomes green, and later breaks into blue tears. Students can explain why each visual decision helps the reader understand an otherwise abstract character, then select colour, position and shape purposefully in their own multimodal narrative.

## Reading connections

### Predicting - Foundation
**Key Understanding:** "Predicting is when we think about what might happen in the book."  
**Key Skill:** "I can use the title and front cover to predict what might happen in the text"  
**In this book:** The verified cover shows blue and yellow circles overlapping to make green. Before reading, students can predict whether the colours will meet, mix or change, name the cover evidence supporting the prediction, and revisit it when the friends hug.

### Inferring - Foundation
**Key Understanding:** "Clues within a text are needed to infer the emotions of a character"  
**Key Skill:** "I can infer the emotions of a character using pictures and words (happy, sad, angry, excited, tired)."  
**In this book:** When both families reject the green children, the words say they are sad and the images show the characters breaking into separate blue and yellow tears. Students can combine the parents' speech, the repeated crying and the scattered visual forms to explain the intensity of the children's feelings.

### Inferring - Year 1
**Key Understanding:** "Inferring is when we work out what the author doesn't exactly say."  
**Key Skill:** "I can infer character feelings using text clues such as pictures and words."  
**In this book:** The narration never explains in detail why the parents reject the children or how their thinking changes. Students infer that the adults rely on colour as proof of identity, then revise that belief when their own hug produces green. The repeated home scenes and mirrored transformation provide evidence for an inference about recognition, regret and relief.

### Synthesising - Year 2
**Key Understanding:** "That synthesis is when we use our prior knowledge and the text to build deeper understandings. (Synthesising)"  
**Key Skill:** "I can identify how the new knowledge developed my thinking. (synthesising)"  
**In this book:** A reader may first understand the green overlap as colour mixing. The rejection scenes add an identity problem, and the parents' transformation changes the event again into evidence that appearance can change while relationship and identity continue. Students can record how their understanding shifts at the hug, rejection, tears and reunion.

### Summarising - Foundation
**Key Understanding:** "Retelling is when I tell what happened in the text"  
**Key Skill:** "I can retell the beginning, problem and solution of a text."  
**In this book:** The spare text and visually distinct events make the narrative structure traceable: two friends are introduced; becoming green leads their families not to recognise them; the parents reproduce the colour change and understand. Students can select one image for each part and retell the causal chain without turning every play episode into a main event.

### Analysing - Year 2
**Key Understanding:** "Texts use different language features depending on their purpose."  
**Key Skill:** "I can make connections between language features and their purpose."  
**In this book:** Lionni's purpose depends on words and images working together. Repetition extends searching and crying, direct speech creates the rejection, and the visual overlap supplies information the prose leaves largely unexplained. Students can analyse what each mode contributes and test what meaning would be lost if either the words or illustrations were removed.

## PRIDE values

### Respect - repair after a counterexample
**Ngarri wording:** "We respect ourselves, our school and each other, and understand the importance of treating others with compassion, care and courtesy."  
**In this book:** The parents' refusal to recognise the green children is a counterexample: they judge by changed appearance instead of listening to the children's claim. Respect is repaired when the adults experience the same transformation, welcome both children and share the discovery with the other family. This supports discussion of valuing difference while also checking assumptions and repairing exclusion.

## Inquiry lenses

### Identity, Creativity and Wellbeing
**In this book:** Little blue and little yellow remain themselves, their friendship changes their appearance, and others initially fail to recognise them. Their distress and eventual welcome make identity relational as well as visual. Students can inquire into what can change about a person, what remains, who gets to recognise identity, and how belonging affects wellbeing.

### The Physical World
**In this book:** The central visual proposition is that blue and yellow overlap to produce green. The story provides a useful starting model, not complete scientific proof: students can predict, test and document what happens when transparent colour, paint or paper layers combine, then compare their observations with Lionni's artistic representation and evaluate where the model is accurate or simplified.

## Structured teaching ideas

### 1. Make a prediction from the cover
**Linked focus:** Predicting; Presentation. **Years:** Foundation-1. **Time/grouping/support:** 15 minutes; whole class then partners; high modelling.  
**Where to look:** The verified cover, then the friends' first hug.  
**Learning focus:** Use a visible clue to make and revisit a prediction.  
**Teaching sequence:** Notice the blue-yellow overlap without naming the outcome; model "I predict... because..."; collect two plausible predictions; read to the hug; confirm or revise using the green image.  
**Student application:** Students make an evidence-based prediction from another cover or prepared colour image.  
**Notice learning:** Students point to a specific clue and revise rather than merely mark a prediction right or wrong.

### 2. Retell the causal chain
**Linked focus:** Organisation; Summarising. **Years:** Foundation-2. **Time/grouping/support:** 25 minutes; pairs; moderate support.  
**Where to look:** Introduction, search, transformation, rejection, tears and reunion.  
**Learning focus:** Distinguish main causal events from interesting side episodes.  
**Teaching sequence:** Sequence six verified images; ask what changed because of each event; remove the park-play details and test whether the central story still makes sense; jointly compose a concise oral retell.  
**Student application:** Students plan a beginning-problem-solution narrative with arrows showing cause and effect.  
**Notice learning:** Students preserve the transformation and recognition problem and can justify omitted details.

### 3. Read feelings across words and pictures
**Linked focus:** Inferring; Respect. **Years:** Foundation-2. **Time/grouping/support:** 25 minutes; whole class and pairs; high-to-moderate support.  
**Where to look:** Both rejection scenes, the tears and the parents' green hug.  
**Learning focus:** Combine verbal and visual evidence to infer feelings and changing beliefs.  
**Teaching sequence:** Model an inference from one rejection page; separate what the words say from what the image adds; pairs trace one character's likely feelings across the next three moments; discuss how the adults repair exclusion.  
**Student application:** Students explain a feeling change in another picture book using one word clue and one image clue.  
**Notice learning:** Students cite both modes and avoid treating colour alone as a fixed emotion code.

### 4. Hear how repetition changes pace
**Linked focus:** Word Choice; Sentence Fluency. **Years:** 1-2. **Time/grouping/support:** 20 minutes; whole class then individual; moderate support.  
**Where to look:** "here and there and everywhere", the repeated hugging and repeated crying.  
**Learning focus:** Explain how repetition stretches time, builds rhythm and intensifies feeling.  
**Teaching sequence:** Read each passage once without repetition and once as written; compare pace and effect; mark repeated words and conjunctions; jointly innovate a short searching or celebrating sentence.  
**Student application:** Students revise one action in their own narrative using purposeful repetition.  
**Notice learning:** The repetition has an intended effect and remains fluent when read aloud.

### 5. Test the colour model
**Linked focus:** The Physical World; Synthesising. **Years:** 1-3. **Time/grouping/support:** 40 minutes; small groups; moderate support.  
**Where to look:** Cover, overlapping hug, green character and parents' later transformation.  
**Learning focus:** Treat an illustration as a model that can be predicted, tested and evaluated.  
**Teaching sequence:** Record what the book suggests; test blue and yellow with transparent overlays and paint; gather observations; compare materials; return to the story and identify what the model explains and what it simplifies.  
**Student application:** Students create an annotated before-overlap-after record and write one evidence-based conclusion.  
**Notice learning:** Students distinguish the fictional identity story from observable colour mixing and qualify claims by material.

### 6. What changes and what remains?
**Linked focus:** Identity, Creativity and Wellbeing; Respect. **Years:** 2-4. **Time/grouping/support:** 30 minutes; discussion circle; moderate support.  
**Where to look:** The green transformation, both rejections and the final shared play.  
**Learning focus:** Explore identity without reducing it to appearance.  
**Teaching sequence:** Build two columns, "changed" and "remained"; require evidence for each claim; examine why the parents' first test fails; discuss what later evidence changes their minds and what respectful recognition might require.  
**Student application:** Students create a short multimodal identity piece showing one visible change and two enduring qualities or relationships.  
**Notice learning:** Students separate appearance, action, relationship and identity and can revise an initial assumption.

### 7. Tell a story with minimal shapes
**Linked focus:** Ideas; Presentation; Audience/Voice. **Years:** Foundation-3. **Time/grouping/support:** 45-60 minutes; individual with peer response; moderate support.  
**Where to look:** Home shapes, search positions, full-colour backgrounds, overlap, tears and reunion.  
**Learning focus:** Make deliberate visual choices that carry narrative meaning for an audience.  
**Teaching sequence:** Analyse how scale, position, colour and overlap represent setting, movement and emotion; plan a three-to-six-frame story; draft with paper shapes and one concise sentence per frame; peer-test whether the sequence is understandable; revise one visual choice.  
**Student application:** Publish the revised multimodal narrative for a younger partner class.  
**Notice learning:** Visual changes are purposeful, the sequence is comprehensible, and words add rather than simply duplicate the image.

# Writing useful mentor-text content

Updated: 10 September 2026
Status: approved canonical guide
Purpose: quality standard based on the strongest features of the approved *Crickwing* content and Phill's review

This guide explains how to turn a complete book and the Ngarri curriculum documents into content that a teacher can use. It does not approve or replace an individual book record. Work in small announced batches of one to three books, but complete one book at a time and obtain teacher approval before publishing or beginning the next book.

## The result we are aiming for

A useful entry lets a teacher answer four questions quickly:

1. **Where is the evidence in this particular book?**
2. **What exactly is the author or illustrator doing there?**
3. **How does that example demonstrate the selected Ngarri Key Understanding and Key Skill?**
4. **What could students learn to notice, discuss or try in their own work?**

The explanation must depend on the actual book. If the title can be replaced with another title and the paragraph still works, the entry is too generic.

The purpose is not to attach as many curriculum labels as possible. The purpose is to identify the few or many connections that the book demonstrates especially well and explain them accurately enough that another teacher can teach through the book.

## Why *Crickwing* is the model

The strongest *Crickwing* entries do more than name a curriculum match. Each year-level explanation changes because the curriculum focus changes.

For example, the Organisation entries use different evidence and reasoning:

- **Year 3 — pacing:** the predator encounters and final confrontation show where Cannon slows a moment and where she moves quickly between events.
- **Year 4 — structure:** the rainforest setting, repeated attacks, Crickwing's retaliation, Eartha's intervention and the final rescue form a cohesive narrative that resolves an external danger and a character problem.
- **Year 5 — cohesion through cause and effect:** Crickwing is hurt, he hurts creatures with less power, his choices endanger the colony, and Eartha's compassion gives him an opportunity to repair the harm.
- **Year 6 — purposeful design:** recurring or mirrored elements, including harm and Crickwing's food sculpture, help unite the narrative and turn an earlier interest into part of the solution.

These are not four copies of one plot summary. Each paragraph selects the part of the book that illuminates that year's exact curriculum wording. That is what makes the content useful for planning and teaching.

## Step 1: establish the evidence base

Read the complete text and inspect the illustrations wherever visual meaning is part of the proposed connection. Do not build a book-specific recommendation from a title, catalogue description, online summary or memory.

Before extracting text from a PDF, search the private JimK continuity archive and the Drive extraction folders for an existing transcription or searchable text-only PDF. Check a candidate transcription against the complete school copy, including the beginning, middle, ending and every passage selected as evidence. Record edition differences or uncertain wording. Extract from the PDF only when a suitable checked text does not exist, and use OCR only when the PDF lacks a reliable text layer.

At the same time, check the bibliographic details, year band, displayed cover and school resource links. Confirm that the cover is correct and renders properly. The authenticated site should ultimately offer the original school PDF and a Drive-hosted searchable text-only PDF when available.

Before selecting curriculum connections, make a private evidence map of the book. Record:

- the sequence of important events or ideas;
- changes in characters, relationships, knowledge or point of view;
- problems, choices, consequences, turning points and resolutions;
- recurring words, phrases, images, motifs or structural patterns;
- distinctive vocabulary, sentences, voice, tone and figurative language;
- ways words and illustrations add, withhold or complicate meaning;
- themes, tensions and concepts that the whole work supports; and
- precise internal locators that allow a reviewer to find the evidence again.

Internal evidence records may use a page, spread, section, opening words or event description. Teacher-facing prose should normally refer to the recognisable moment rather than require a particular PDF page number, because editions can differ.

Keep four kinds of material distinguishable: what the book explicitly shows or says, a short quotation, the writer's interpretation, and a newly designed teaching suggestion. The private GitHub repository's `restricted-reference/` directory is an approved continuity archive for text-only transcriptions.

## Step 2: classify the book

Record the broad text type, one or more useful genres and a short rationale based on the complete work.

- **Text type** describes the book's broad communicative form, such as narrative, informative, persuasive, or a justified hybrid.
- **Genre** describes the more specific literary or informational form, such as realistic fiction, trickster tale, lyrical family story or narrative nonfiction.
- **Rationale** points to the features of the whole book that justify the classification.

Classification is part of the reviewable teaching content. Do not treat a publisher's marketing label as sufficient evidence, and do not force a book into a single genre when two or three precise labels clarify how it works.

## Step 3: select only strong connections

Compare the evidence map with the exact Ngarri documents below. Select a connection only when the book provides a clear, teachable example of the curriculum focus.

Use these repository files as the primary matching sources:

- `reference-data/writing-throughlines.json` for the seven Foundation–Year 6 writing traits, Key Understandings and Key Skills;
- `reference-data/reading-strategies.json` for the Foundation–Year 6 comprehension strategies, Key Understandings and Key Skills;
- `reference-data/pride-values.json` for the Ngarri PRIDE concepts and behaviours; and
- `reference-data/inquiry-lenses.json` for the four Ngarri conceptual inquiry lenses.

Use `curriculum-review-2026-09-06/curriculum-reference-records.json` when a connection comes from the later supplied English or inquiry documents. Check its `source-manifest.json` and `review-verification.json` so the source and extraction status remain visible. The school-owned originals preserved in `reference-sources/school-originals/` are provenance and verification copies; consult the relevant original when a structured record is incomplete or uncertain.

Professional readings may support pedagogy or the documented missing-Key-Skill exception, but they do not replace Ngarri wording. Name the exact professional source and distinguish its wording from school curriculum wording. `reference-data/README.md` remains the authority map for this reference set.

There is no quota. A book may have several excellent writing traits and reading strategies and no convincing example of another. It is better to omit a weak connection than to make the library appear complete with generic content.

Use these selection tests:

- Is there a precise moment, pattern or craft choice to examine?
- Does it demonstrate the nominated curriculum idea particularly well?
- Can the explanation show how or why, rather than merely assert a match?
- Would a teacher know what to draw students' attention to?
- Is this connection distinct from the other selected connections?

## Step 4: write every writing entry for its exact year level

Organise writing content as:

1. writing trait;
2. year levels from lowest to highest;
3. exact Key Understanding for that year;
4. matching exact Key Skill for that year; and
5. a new, book-specific explanation written for that particular pair.

Copy the Key Understanding and Key Skill exactly from the nominated Ngarri source. Preserve the source name and version. Do not modernise, combine, paraphrase or move curriculum wording between years without making that change explicit and seeking review.

Write each explanation separately, even when several years sit under the same trait. A useful short explanation usually contains four moves:

1. **Locate:** name the exact scene, passage, pattern or visual feature.
2. **Explain the craft:** state what the author or illustrator does.
3. **Connect:** explain why that craft demonstrates the selected Key Understanding and Key Skill.
4. **Teach and transfer:** state what students can notice and what they might try, explain or create themselves.

These moves can form one concise paragraph; they do not need four headings in the teacher interface.

### What to look for within writing traits

- **Ideas:** the source of an idea, its focus, supporting detail, elaboration, viewpoint and development. Name the particular idea and show how the book develops it.
- **Organisation:** sequence, pacing, text structure, cohesion, transitions, repeated patterns, openings, endings and the relationships among parts. Explain how the arrangement creates meaning or effect.
- **Word Choice:** precise vocabulary, verbs, sensory detail, figurative language, repetition, connotation and patterns of language. Identify the actual language feature and its effect.
- **Audience/Voice:** narrator, tone, mood, register, stance, address to the reader and changes across relationships or purposes. Show how language positions or engages the reader.
- **Sentence Fluency:** sentence length, rhythm, fragments, punctuation, repetition and variation. Explain how the sentence pattern supports pace, emphasis, voice or meaning.
- **Presentation:** layout, typography and the relationship between visual and verbal modes. Use only features verified in the edition examined.
- **Conventions:** purposeful spelling, punctuation, grammar, paragraphing or dialogue conventions. Select examples that teach meaning and control, not isolated proofreading exercises.

Only include a trait when the book is a strong mentor for it.

## Step 5: write reading entries with the same precision

Organise reading content as:

1. reading strategy;
2. applicable year levels from lowest to highest;
3. exact Key Understanding;
4. matching exact Key Skill; and
5. a separate book-specific explanation for each year-level pair.

The explanation should identify the evidence a reader must use and the thinking the selected strategy requires. It should make clear why this book offers a rich opportunity for that strategy.

Examples of the distinction:

- **Predicting** requires a point before an outcome and clues that make more than a random guess possible.
- **Inferring** requires readers to combine particular textual or visual clues with relevant knowledge to reach and justify an interpretation.
- **Synthesising** requires an initial understanding that changes or becomes more complex as later information is integrated.
- **Determining importance or summarising** requires readers to decide which events or ideas carry the meaning and which details can be omitted.
- **Analysing** requires attention to how parts of the text work together or how an author or illustrator shapes meaning.
- **Visualising** requires language or sound patterns that support the construction and revision of a mental image.
- **Making connections** requires readers to connect the text with their experience, another text or the world in a way that deepens understanding. It does not mean characters connecting with one another.
- **Questioning** requires worthwhile uncertainties that can guide attention, interpretation or further inquiry.

If a trusted professional reading provides a matching Key Understanding but no Key Skill, the connection may be proposed with the Key Skill left empty. Name the source and never invent curriculum wording. The approved *Night Tree* Visualising entry is the current example.

## Step 6: write PRIDE values as concepts

PRIDE connections are not divided into year levels. Select a value only when a specific choice, relationship, action or consequence helps students understand the concept.

Each explanation should:

- identify the relevant moment or pattern;
- explain how the value is demonstrated, tested, absent or repaired;
- acknowledge complexity rather than labelling a character as simply good or bad; and
- give the teacher a useful conceptual distinction to discuss.

For example, *Crickwing* can support discussion of respect through the contrast between humiliation, retaliation and Eartha's response. Respect can include recognising another creature's dignity while still taking harm seriously; it does not require pretending that harmful behaviour is acceptable.

Counterexamples must be stored and displayed as counterexamples. A harmful action should not create a positive value tag merely because it prompts a discussion about that value.

## Step 7: write inquiry connections around themes and tensions

Inquiry lenses are not divided into year levels. Begin with a concept, tension or important question that is genuinely carried by the book, then explain how particular events or choices make it available for inquiry.

A strong inquiry entry:

- names the concept or lens;
- grounds it in more than one meaningful part of the book;
- identifies a tension that is worth thinking about rather than supplying a moral; and
- shows how the text can open inquiry without turning the book into a compulsory full unit.

Avoid broad claims such as “this book teaches relationships” unless the explanation identifies which relationships change, what causes the change and what conceptual question students can investigate.

## Step 8: turn rich connections into structured teaching ideas

Teaching ideas belong after the curriculum, PRIDE and inquiry sections. They can expand the strongest book connections into classroom-ready practice.

Each idea should include:

- **Title:** a short description of the learning, not just the activity.
- **Linked focus:** the relevant trait, strategy, value or inquiry lens.
- **Suggested years:** a realistic range informed by the curriculum match and text demands.
- **Time, grouping and level of support:** enough information for planning.
- **Where to look:** the exact moment, passage, pattern or verified illustration.
- **Learning focus:** what students are learning about reading, writing or a concept.
- **Teaching sequence:** what the teacher and students do with the book, in a sensible order.
- **Student application:** how students transfer the learning to their own reading, talk, writing or inquiry.
- **Notice learning:** observable evidence that shows whether students understand the focus.

The sequence should begin with the book's actual language, structure, event or image. A generic routine such as “pause and predict” becomes useful only after it names the precise pause point, the clues available there, the plausible predictions those clues support and how later evidence changes the reading.

Do not leave placeholder student applications or repeat one task under several curriculum labels. One well-designed activity can link to several approved connections when the relationships are explicit.

### Use relevant education skills deliberately

Before drafting teaching ideas, inspect the installed education skills and use the narrowest relevant skill or combination of skills. For example:

- `reading-comprehension-strategy-selector` can check that a reading strategy serves the intended understanding and that the before-, during- and after-reading moves are purposeful;
- `pedagogical-content-knowledge-developer` can identify likely misconceptions, useful representations and observable evidence of understanding; and
- `think-aloud-script-generator` can help make an expert reader's or writer's decisions visible when teacher modelling is part of the activity.

Record the skill names used and what they shaped in the draft's provenance notes. If no suitable installed education skill exists, use the available skill-discovery or installer workflow to look for one before drafting the teaching examples.

Skill output is design support, not book or curriculum evidence. Check every suggestion against the complete book, the nominated Ngarri reference and this guide. Reject generic activities, invented quotations and sequences that do not begin with verified evidence from the book.

## A diagnostic example of what needs improvement

In the current *Alphabet Tree* Organisation records, the same general explanation is repeated across Years 2–5: the plot embodies a hierarchy of written language and each new level solves the previous problem while creating a larger purpose. That is an interesting whole-book observation, but repetition does not explain the different curriculum expectations.

A revision would need to examine each year afresh:

- the Year 2 entry would explain the book's logical sequence using exact events;
- the Year 3 entry would connect its particular structure and language features with its purpose;
- the Year 4 entry would identify and explain the organisational features the curriculum names; and
- the Year 5 entry would analyse the actual devices that create cohesion across the text.

This diagnostic does not decide the final connections or wording. Those require a fresh complete-text review and will be presented as a separate book draft.

## Quality checks before a book reaches the reviewer

Every proposed book must pass these checks:

- The complete text was read, and visual claims were checked against the actual illustrations.
- Text type, genre and rationale are present.
- Every writing and reading connection uses the exact sourced Key Understanding and matching Key Skill, or clearly records the approved no-skill exception.
- Every year-level explanation is written for that year's curriculum pair.
- Every explanation names evidence unique enough to locate in the book.
- Every explanation says how the evidence demonstrates the curriculum focus.
- The prose gives a teacher a practical pathway into noticing, discussion or student transfer.
- No paragraph is reused across different year levels merely because the trait or strategy is the same.
- Weak connections have been omitted; the book has not been padded to meet a quota.
- PRIDE and inquiry entries are concept-specific and grounded in events, choices or patterns.
- Counterexamples are identified accurately.
- Teaching ideas have all relevant structured fields, including student application and observable learning.
- Relevant installed education skills were used for teaching-idea design, or the skill-discovery check and its result were recorded.
- Book facts, curriculum quotations, interpretations and designed teaching ideas remain distinguishable.
- Existing JimK and Drive text resources were checked before new extraction or OCR was attempted.
- The title, author, illustrator, publication details, year band, cover and school resource-link status were checked.
- Resource links and publishing locations were checked against the school's current access decision.
- All new records remain `ai_suggested` until the teacher records approval.

A useful final test is: **Could a teacher who knows the curriculum but has not planned this book see exactly what to revisit, what to teach and why this book is worth choosing?**

## Review and improvement cycle

1. Prepare one complete book draft from verified source material.
2. Run the quality checks and flag any uncertain evidence rather than smoothing it over.
3. Present the draft to Phill as `ai_suggested`.
4. Record approval, rejection or requested revision without overwriting the original review history.
5. Publish only approved records to the teacher-facing library.
6. Verify the approved Supabase records and authenticated preview page, including the cover and resource links.
7. Update this guide when a reviewed book reveals a general rule that will improve future books.
8. Move to the next book only after the previous book has been decided.

The five-book trial is complete. *Crickwing*, *The Alphabet Tree*, *Night Tree*, *Little Blue and Little Yellow* and *The Gruffalo* form the approved quality benchmark. *Fox* remains a separate calibration example.

## Inquiry possibility: relationships and choices

**Candidate school lens:** Identity, Creativity and Wellbeing. The recovered lens definition includes healthy relationships and wise choices. The book connection remains the relationship candidate documented in the original review; this addendum adds no newly verified scene claims.

**Possible conceptual intention:** Students explore how choices can affect trust and relationships, and consider different perspectives.

**Suggested question:** How can our choices strengthen or damage a friendship?

This question is a draft for teacher consideration, not an official school question or a quotation from Murdoch. Students might raise a different question worth pursuing.

**Tuning In possibility, about 10 minutes after an initial reading:** Ask students what they wonder about the relationships. Collect questions without immediately answering them. Group related questions together and invite students to explain which they would like to explore. Notice different interpretations and let these inform the next lesson.

**Sorting Out possibility, about 15–20 minutes:** Revisit two teacher-selected moments. Partners record a character choice, the evidence they can point to, and their interpretation of its effect on the relationship. Compare interpretations: which are supported, where do students disagree, and what further evidence would help? Do not treat a plausible interpretation as a directly stated fact.

**Listen for:** Students distinguish what happened from what they think it means, use evidence, and consider another interpretation. This is evidence of their thinking, not a test of whether they repeat a teacher's preferred moral.

**Possible continuation:** If students' questions justify it, compare another text or examine a hypothetical friendship dilemma. Invite reflection on how their thinking changed. A personal disclosure, action project or complete five-phase unit is not required.

**Review:** Is the lens connection strong? Is the question open and useful for this class? Which moments support the activity? Approve, edit, reject or pending: ______

## Reading: choose the appropriate pathway

### A. Year 3 — revise a prediction

**Exact supplied Key Skill:** “I can confirm or modify predictions using evidence.”

Source: Comprehension Throughlines, table 3, Year Three; reference ID `Comprehension_Throughlines_Victorian_English_2.0:table3:column4`.

**Learning intention:** We are learning to change or keep a prediction for a reason we can explain.

**Activity, about 15 minutes within a first reading:** At a teacher-selected stopping point, students predict what might happen and identify a clue. Read further, then revisit predictions. Students explain what they kept or changed and identify the new evidence. If students already know the ending, choose an unfamiliar text for assessing prediction; do not ask them to pretend they do not know.

**Notice learning:** The student explains a revision or confirmation using relevant evidence. Guessing the ending correctly is not the success criterion.

### B. Year 4 — infer a character's perspective

**Exact supplied Key Skill:** “I can infer character traits, motives and perspectives using evidence.”

Source: Comprehension Throughlines, table 3, Year Four; reference ID `Comprehension_Throughlines_Victorian_English_2.0:table3:column5`.

**Learning intention:** We are learning to explain an interpretation of a character using clues.

**Activity, about 20 minutes:** Revisit a teacher-selected spread. Model separating observation from interpretation. Partners suggest a character's possible motive or perspective, identify supporting clues, and discuss another possible interpretation. Ask whether the evidence makes one explanation more convincing or leaves uncertainty.

**Notice learning:** Students support an inference with relevant words or image details and can consider an alternative.

The original R1 uses a different recovered Year 3 reference. Preserve that attribution. Do not present its wording as a quotation from the new Year 3 column. These pathways are curriculum planning options; they do not impose a universal reading level for Fox.

## Speaking and listening: build on a partner's thinking

**Exact supplied Year 3 Key Skill:** “I can confirm, build on and prompt others' ideas.”

Source: Speaking and Listening Throughlines, table 1, Year Three; reference ID `Speaking_and_Listening_Throughlines_Victorian_English_2.0:table1:column4`.

Use this as a deliberate focus within the discussion above. Model “I would add … because …” and “What makes you think …?” Students practise responding to a partner's idea before adding their own. Listen for a response that actually connects to what the partner said. Sentence frames are optional scaffolds, not the curriculum wording or a checklist of correct phrases.

## Vocabulary: investigate meaning in context

**Exact supplied Year 3 Key Skill:** “I can use context and word parts to infer meaning.”

Source: Phonics, Fluency, Decoding and Vocabulary Throughlines, table 4, Year Three; reference ID `Phonics_Fluency_Decoding_Vocabulary_Throughlines_Condensed_Same_Format:table4:column4`.

**Draft activity, about 10 minutes:** Select an unfamiliar word from the actual text. Students propose a meaning using the surrounding language; examine useful word parts where the selected word supports this. Check the meaning and reread to see whether it fits. If no suitable word-part example is available, record that the activity practises the contextual part of the skill only. Do not invent words or claim that all parts of the skill were demonstrated.

**Notice learning:** Students explain which clues helped and revise an initial interpretation where necessary. Passage and word selection remain pending.

## Fluency: use the voice to convey an interpretation

**Exact supplied Year 3 Key Skill:** “I can adjust pace, pitch, tone and expression using punctuation.”

Source: Phonics, Fluency, Decoding and Vocabulary Throughlines, table 3, Year Three; reference ID `Phonics_Fluency_Decoding_Vocabulary_Throughlines_Condensed_Same_Format:table3:column4`.

**Draft activity, about 10 minutes:** Choose a short, accessible excerpt from the classroom copy. Model phrasing, then let students rehearse an expressive reading with support. Compare two readings and discuss how punctuation and meaning informed the choices. Students explain one adjustment. This is supported excerpt practice; no claim is made that the whole book is independently readable or decodable for Year 3.

**Notice learning:** A student's phrasing or expression responds to punctuation and their understanding of the passage. Excerpt selection remains pending.

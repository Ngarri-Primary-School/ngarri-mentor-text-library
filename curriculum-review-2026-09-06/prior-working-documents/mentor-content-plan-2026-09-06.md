# Ngarri Mentor Text Library: creating the teaching content

Working plan for Phill — 6 September 2026

## The outcome

Each book page should help a teacher answer: **Why would I choose this book, what could I teach with it, and how could I start?** A useful page can have a few strong connections. It does not need a connection to every framework.

The Fox screenshots provide the existing visual example. We will use them to organise and evaluate content before expanding across the collection. Their example teaching claims are prototype material awaiting source checking, not approved evidence about the book.

## What we already have

The restored database contains 343 books, 391 writing annotations and 260 reading annotations. Those annotations belong to 90 books: 54 have both kinds and 36 have writing only. The other 253 have neither. Counts of tags do not establish that their book-specific explanations are complete.

Ngarri's reference records contain seven writing traits, eleven reading strategies, five PRIDE values and four inquiry lenses, with writing and reading year-level detail. We must verify the reference wording against the supplied school documents before using it as the basis for new alignments. These are reusable school definitions; they should not be regenerated separately for each book.

PRIDE annotations, inquiry annotations, teaching ideas, why-use bullets and teaching resources are currently empty. Covers and the six blurbs marked for review also need attention. These gaps should be tracked separately from curriculum enrichment.

## What a book record needs

| Part of the page | What supplies it | What AI contributes | What the reviewer checks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Book identity and blurb | Existing catalogue plus verified publisher/author sources | Helps find and compare sources; does not invent a blurb | Correct title, author, illustrator and edition; description matches the actual book |
| Writing throughlines | Ngarri's exact year-level Key Understanding and Key Skill; existing teacher/OzLit tags | Drafts an explanation of the particular writing craft in this book | A demonstrable technique and appropriate curriculum match, not just a plot theme |
| Reading comprehension | Ngarri's exact strategy and year-level wording; existing teacher/OzLit tags | Explains where and why a reader needs the strategy | Understanding the text actually benefits from this strategy; the connection is specific |
| PRIDE values | Ngarri's value definitions | Connects a value to a particular decision or event | The event supports the value; a vague theme is insufficient |
| Inquiry lenses | Ngarri's lens definitions and exact Big Questions | Explains how the book opens up a particular question | The actual Big Question fits; a shared keyword alone is insufficient |
| Teaching ideas | Strong, checked book connections | Drafts practical activities with time, grouping and intended learning | A teacher could use the idea; it matches the book and learning intention |
| Why use this book? | The approved connections above | Condenses them into three to five brief reasons | Every reason is supported elsewhere on the page |
| Resources and related books | Checked resource links and existing collection records | Can help identify candidates | Resource exists; related title is in the collection; relationship is meaningful |

Show the strongest connections first. The earlier field specification suggests one to three writing/reading connections and one to two PRIDE/inquiry connections. Treat these as presentation guidance, not quotas or a reason to discard additional well-supported connections. Blank is acceptable when evidence is insufficient.

## Fox: the first worked example

The saved screenshots propose Voice/Audience, Word Choice and Organisation; Inferring; Determination; and the Identity, Creativity & Wellbeing lens. These are starting candidates, not fresh conclusions from this audit.

For **Voice/Audience**, the prototype describes how word choices sustain a mood. The completed review item should put the exact Ngarri year-level wording beside a located passage or verified source, followed by a short explanation of how the language demonstrates the skill. A reviewer must be able to check the connection against the book.

For **Inferring**, we should identify a particular moment where the reader works out something that is not stated directly. The review record needs the relevant text/image evidence and a question that directs students to it. We should not assign the strategy simply because readers can infer something from most stories.

For **Determination**, identify the actual character decision or event, then compare it with Ngarri's definition. For **Identity, Creativity & Wellbeing**, copy the relevant Big Question from the school reference and explain how the story supports exploring it. Do not invent a substitute question and present it as school curriculum.

The prototype's **Spotlight Writing** activity is a useful draft candidate. Before approval, check the scene it refers to and whether its account of the author's technique is accurate. Then make the activity concrete: learning intention, passage to revisit, teacher modelling, student task, approximate time and a simple way to notice learning.

Access to the actual book, a lawful preview or an adequate published source is needed to verify these claims. A screenshot of an AI explanation is not evidence that the explanation is true. Fox is not currently a standalone title in the recovered 343-book catalogue; use it as a separate design/calibration example unless its addition to the school collection is confirmed.

## How content will be created

1. **Check the reusable school references.** Confirm exact wording, year levels and naming against the source documents. Record and resolve extraction discrepancies once. Separate school quotations from derived selection guidance.
2. **Create a gap list for every book.** Distinguish missing tags, missing explanations, unverified blurbs, absent covers and fields genuinely reviewed as not applicable. Preserve every existing teacher/OzLit tag and its source.
3. **Collect evidence before drafting.** Confirm book identity. Save source details and short supporting excerpts or passage/page references where available. A publisher blurb may establish plot but may not demonstrate writing craft. If evidence is too thin, send the item for human input.
4. **Draft each connection separately.** AI uses the verified school references and the evidence collected for that book. It proposes a connection, explains why, suggests a year level only when justified, and records uncertainty. Contradictions with existing tags are flagged for review, never silently overwritten.
5. **Draft teaching ideas from supported connections.** Aim initially for two or three worthwhile ideas. A generic strategy activity must be identified as generic to the reviewer and must not make invented claims about scenes. Generate the why-use summary last.
6. **Review and save the exact decision.** Phill or a designated reviewer can approve, edit, reject or request more evidence for each item. Approval of a writing explanation does not automatically approve the book's other sections. Save the original suggestion, reviewed version, reviewer and date.
7. **Publish approved items.** The teacher page displays approved teaching content and the exact school curriculum wording. Draft suggestions remain available through the future secured reviewer area. The database restriction is already in place; the review interface is not yet built. A structured review file can support the first trial.

## Prove the method before scaling

First complete Fox as a calibration example. Then establish a permanent 20-book benchmark from the real collection, including different text types, age ranges, evidence quality and some books for which few or no connections are justified.

The old notes describe a 20-book writing/reading trial, but its saved responses and review file have not been recovered. Do not claim a new selection is the same sample or that it inherits previous approval. If the originals are recovered, retain them and document any added categories; otherwise save a newly selected list with identifiers and selection reasons. Including tagged books in the benchmark tests explanations and preservation of existing work, not permission to replace their tags.

For each trial item, record whether book facts are correct, curriculum wording is exact, the connection has evidence, the year level makes sense and the classroom suggestion is useful. Track how much editing is required and why items fail. Correct recurring problems and rerun affected cases. Phill reviews the complete benchmark and explicitly approves the method before a collection-wide run; automatic format checks alone cannot establish educational quality.

The first broad writing/reading tagging pass remains focused on the 253 books with neither kind of tag, as the existing project plan specifies. The 36 writing-only books and missing explanations on already-tagged books need a separately tracked follow-up. PRIDE, inquiry, activities and summaries are considered across all 343 books, with no expectation that all categories will suit all books.
